Talking about money with kids ages 4–8

Start with one small everyday choice. You do not need to explain every part of your family finances: listen to one question and think it through together.

Before you begin

Choose a calm moment. Use paper and pencils to draw two choices, with no real money needed. You do not need the book or printed materials.

A conversation in three steps

  1. Ask: “If we could choose one thing to do together, what would it be?” Listen before offering your own ideas.
  2. Draw one choice for today and another that could wait. Explain: “When we choose one thing, sometimes we leave something else for later.”
  3. Ask: “What would help us get ready?” Suggest one small step, such as putting aside a counter or drawing the goal.

An example to try

Your child wants a toy and a trip to the park. Draw both ideas and talk about which needs money and which needs time or preparation. The aim is to explain a choice, without judging the wish.

When a question feels difficult

You can say “I do not know; let us think about it” or “We cannot buy that today.” You do not have to share adult money worries or promise a future purchase.

To finish

Ask: “Which idea would you like to remember?” Younger children can answer with one word or a drawing. Older children can discuss what might change their choice.
