Saving activity: one container, one goal and one drawing

Make one small goal visible and notice progress together. Try this without buying the book, printing anything or using real money.

Materials

A safe, unbreakable container, paper, pencils and large paper counters. An adult handles any cutting. Avoid coins and small pieces around children who may put them in their mouths.

Step by step

  1. Choose one small, specific goal: saving for an object or preparing a family activity. Agree what each counter means if you are not using money.
  2. Draw the goal and place it on the container. If using money, an adult can help show what is left; with counters, mark the agreed steps.
  3. Choose a time to review progress. Ask “What changed?” and “Do we still want this goal?” Avoid comparing progress with other children.

An example

For an imaginary goal of ten units, draw ten spaces and color one for every unit saved. If using counters, explain that they are part of the activity and do not automatically represent money or a promised purchase.

When there is no progress

Noticing still matters. Review the drawing and decide whether to change the goal. You do not need to add money every day or reward every household task.

Questions to share

“What do you like about this goal?”, “What could wait?” and “How will we know we are ready?” The adult helps; the child can change their mind.
