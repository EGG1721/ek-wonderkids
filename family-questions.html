Saving, waiting and helping: questions for family reading

Use these questions with any story about everyday choices. If you do not have a book handy, try an imaginary situation and listen to your child’s ideas.

How to use the questions

Choose one question at a time. Pause if your child loses interest. This is a conversation, with no answer to memorize. No materials are needed.

Saving

Ask: “What would the character like to be ready for?” Follow with “What small step could they take today?” Accept a drawing or an everyday example.

Waiting

Ask: “What could wait, and what needs attention today?” Discuss a need, a wish and a surprise without making fun of what your child wants.

Helping

Ask: “How could they help someone?” Helping can mean listening, sharing time or asking an adult for support; it does not always require money.

A situation without a book

Imagine a family wants to prepare a picnic, but rain begins to fall. What do they need now? What could wait? Who might help? Each person can suggest a different solution.

Bring it into everyday life

Choose one idea to try together. Another day, ask: “What happened when we tried it?” Avoid turning the conversation into a promise of results.
